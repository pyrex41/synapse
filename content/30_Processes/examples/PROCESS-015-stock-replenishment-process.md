## Purpose

The stock replenishment process defines how the inventory platform detects low-stock conditions and coordinates the procurement and receiving workflow to restore stock levels above defined thresholds. This process ensures that replenishment signals are timely, accurate, and traceable to the purchasing system.

## Scope

- All active SKUs with configured reorder points across all warehouse locations
- Both automated replenishment signals generated by the inventory platform and manual replenishment requests initiated by operations staff
- Supplier purchase orders from initial creation through receiving confirmation and stock record update
- Does not cover emergency procurement outside the standard supplier relationships

## Roles and Responsibilities

- **Inventory Platform (automated)**: Monitors stock levels continuously and generates replenishment signals when reorder points are reached
- **Procurement Coordinator**: Reviews replenishment signals, raises purchase orders with approved suppliers, and tracks order status
- **Warehouse Receiving Team**: Accepts incoming stock, performs quality checks, and records receipt in the system
- **Inventory Data Analyst**: Validates that received quantities match purchase order and resolves discrepancies before committing to inventory
- **Supplier Relationship Manager**: Escalates delivery delays and coordinates with suppliers on order fulfillment

## Triggers

- System-generated alert when a SKU's available quantity falls to or below its reorder point
- Manual replenishment request raised by warehouse operations for seasonal or event-driven stock needs
- Anticipated demand event (promotional campaign, seasonal peak) flagged by the product team

## Inputs

- Replenishment signal from the inventory platform including SKU, warehouse, current quantity, and recommended order quantity
- Supplier catalog with current pricing and lead times for the flagged SKUs
- Purchase order approval limits per procurement coordinator role

## Outputs

- Approved purchase order sent to supplier
- Inbound shipment record created in the inventory platform
- Stock record updated with received quantities and linked to the purchase order
- Discrepancy report if received quantity differs from purchase order

## Steps

1. Procurement Coordinator receives replenishment signal notification and reviews recommended order quantity against demand forecast and supplier minimum order quantities
2. Create a draft purchase order in the procurement system for the recommended quantity, selecting the preferred supplier based on lead time and price
3. Obtain purchase order approval per authorization limits; orders above threshold require manager approval
4. Submit confirmed purchase order to supplier via EDI or supplier portal and record expected delivery date in the inventory platform as an inbound shipment
5. Warehouse Receiving Team accepts delivery, counts received units, and records receipt against the inbound shipment record
6. Inventory Data Analyst validates received quantities against purchase order; flag discrepancies greater than 1% for supplier credit or recount
7. On receipt validation approval, commit received quantities to the inventory record via the standard receiving API
8. Close the purchase order in the procurement system and update the replenishment signal to resolved

## Controls

- Purchase orders must not be created without a linked replenishment signal or approved manual request
- Received quantities must be validated against purchase order before stock is committed to available inventory
- Replenishment signals that remain open for more than the supplier's stated lead time plus 48 hours must be escalated
- All receiving transactions must be traceable to a purchase order and a physical delivery record
